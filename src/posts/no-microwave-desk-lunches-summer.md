In summer, the office microwave queue gets longer and the appetite for a steaming bowl of leftovers gets shorter. Plenty of people end up buying lunch every day, or eating a sad handful of crackers at their desk.

There's a better middle ground: lunches designed to be eaten cold, that you can pack in a few minutes, and that hold up until 1pm. This post has 15 ideas, plus the food-safety habits that matter more in hot weather than any recipe.

## First: keep cold food cold

Warm weather is when packed lunches can go wrong. Food safety authorities in Australia advise keeping cold food at **5°C or below**, and describe the range between 5°C and 60°C as the "temperature danger zone", where bacteria can grow. A lunch sitting on a desk or in a hot car all morning can spend a long time in that zone.

Simple habits that help:

- **Use the fridge if there is one.** Put your lunch in as soon as you arrive.
- **No fridge? Use an insulated lunch bag with an ice brick,** or a frozen water bottle, which doubles as a cold drink later.
- **Pack straight from the fridge.** Don't leave lunch on the bench while you get ready.
- **Keep the bag out of the sun** and out of the car during the day.
- **Be careful with leftover rice and pasta.** Cool cooked rice quickly, refrigerate it promptly, and keep it cold. Food safety guidance highlights cooked rice as a food to handle carefully.
- **When in doubt, throw it out.** If something has been warm for a while or smells off, don't eat it.

## The formula that stops a cold lunch being boring

A satisfying cold lunch usually has four parts:

1. **Something filling**: grains, pasta, bread, noodles, potatoes or legumes.
2. **Protein**: chicken, tinned fish, eggs, tofu, beans, cheese or leftover meat.
3. **Crunch and freshness**: raw veg, herbs, salad leaves, cucumber, apple.
4. **Big flavour**: a dressing, pickles, chilli, lemon, a good sauce.

Pack the dressing separately so things don't go soggy, and you're most of the way there.

## Salads that hold up until lunchtime

1. **Lemony chickpea salad**: tinned chickpeas, cucumber, tomato, red onion, parsley, feta and a lemon-olive oil dressing. Gets better after a few hours.
2. **Pasta salad with pesto**: short pasta, cherry tomatoes, baby spinach, a spoon of pesto and some shaved parmesan. Add leftover chicken if you have it.
3. **Soba noodle salad**: cold soba, shredded carrot, edamame, spring onion and a soy, sesame and lime dressing.
4. **Mexican-style bean salad**: black beans, corn, capsicum, coriander, lime and a pinch of chilli. Add avocado at the last minute.
5. **Potato and egg salad**: cooked potatoes, boiled egg, peas, dill or chives, and a yoghurt-mustard dressing instead of all mayonnaise.

## Wraps, rolls and sandwiches that aren't soggy by noon

6. **The sturdier sandwich**: use bread rolls or sourdough, spread butter or hummus to the edges as a moisture barrier, and pack tomato separately.
7. **Wraps rolled tight**: tortillas with leftover roast chicken, lettuce, grated carrot and a smear of tzatziki. Wrap in baking paper and cut in half.
8. **Rice paper rolls**: lettuce, vermicelli, herbs, carrot and prawns or tofu, with a dipping sauce in a small container. Cover with a damp paper towel so they don't dry out.
9. **Pita pockets**: falafel, hummus, tabouli and pickles packed separately and assembled at your desk.

## Snack-plate lunches (for grazers)

10. **The desk picnic**: crackers, cheese, sliced cold meats or marinated tofu, cherry tomatoes, cucumber, olives and fruit.
11. **Dip box**: hummus or tzatziki with carrot, capsicum, snow peas, pita chips and a boiled egg or two.
12. **Tinned fish plate**: a tin of tuna or salmon, crackers or rye bread, sliced cucumber and a wedge of lemon.

## Make-ahead jars and bowls

13. **Overnight oats** for a late breakfast-lunch: oats, milk or yoghurt, fruit and seeds, soaked in the fridge overnight.
14. **Grain bowl**: cold quinoa, brown rice or farro (cooled and refrigerated promptly), roasted veg from last night, leafy greens, a protein and a dressing on the side.
15. **Layered jar salad**: dressing at the bottom, then sturdy veg, grains, protein, and leaves on top. Shake or tip into a bowl when you're ready.

## A packing routine that takes ten minutes a week

- **Sunday:** cook one batch of grains or pasta and a protein (boiled eggs, a tray of roast chicken or tofu), cool them quickly and refrigerate.
- **Chop a container of crunchy veg** for the first few days.
- **Make one jar of dressing** to use through the week.
- **Each night:** spend five minutes combining parts into a container, and put it in the fridge with the ice brick in the freezer.

Batch-cooked food is best used within a few days. For a full working week, cook twice, or freeze some portions.

## Keep your desk crumb-free

A lunch at your desk is fine, but your keyboard won't thank you for it.

- **Step away from the screen** to eat if you can, even for ten minutes. It's a better break.
- **Use a placemat or tray** if you eat at your desk.
- **Pick less crumbly options** on busy days: salads and bowls over flaky pastries.
- **Wipe down afterwards.** Desks gather more crumbs and spills than we'd like to think.

Cold lunches don't have to be a compromise. With a good dressing and an ice brick, they might just be the best part of a hot workday.
