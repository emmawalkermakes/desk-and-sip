The office Secret Santa draw has happened, and you've pulled the name of someone from accounts you've spoken to precisely twice. Classic.

The good news: a desk is a pretty reliable clue. Almost everyone who works at one can use something that makes their day a little easier, a little tidier or a little more pleasant. Here are 21 ideas sorted by type, plus a few to avoid.

**First, a quick reality check:** stick to the spending limit your office has set. Going well over it makes other people feel awkward, and going well under can look like you forgot. If there's no limit, ask the organiser.

## If they always have a drink on the go

1. **A good insulated tumbler or bottle.** It keeps iced drinks cold and coffee warm through back-to-back meetings. Check that the lid seals properly if they carry it in a bag.
2. **A set of coasters.** Cork, ceramic or fabric all work. They're useful for anyone who puts sweating iced drinks next to a keyboard.
3. **Nice loose-leaf tea or a reusable tea infuser.** It's a small upgrade from the office box of tea bags.
4. **Locally roasted coffee beans or ground coffee.** If you don't know how they make coffee, ask a teammate, or choose a plunger grind, which suits a lot of home methods.
5. **A mug warmer.** Good for people who make a tea and then forget about it for an hour. Check that it has an automatic shut-off.

## If their desk is… lived in

6. **A desk organiser or tray.** Choose something simple and neutral, such as timber, metal or a plain colour.
7. **A cable tidy kit.** Clips, sleeves or ties. Hardly glamorous, but genuinely appreciated.
8. **A small catch-all dish** for keys, AirPods, lip balm and paper clips.
9. **A good notebook.** Dotted, lined or plain. A nice notebook gets used far more often than a novelty one.
10. **A pen that's genuinely nice to write with.** Most people have never bought themselves one.

## If they're glued to a screen

11. **A screen-cleaning kit** with a microfibre cloth. Check that it's suitable for screens.
12. **A laptop stand.** If budget allows, it's one of the most useful things for anyone working from a laptop. Check whether it folds flat for carrying.
13. **A wrist rest** for keyboard or mouse.
14. **A desk timer** (the flip or cube kind) for people who like to work in focused blocks or need a reminder to stand up.

## If you know nothing about them at all

15. **A hardy desk plant.** Pick something forgiving, like a ZZ plant, snake plant or pothos, ideally in a pot with drainage and a saucer. A plant may not suit everyone's desk, so keep the receipt.
16. **Fancy hand cream.** Air-conditioned offices are drying. Choose something unscented or lightly scented.
17. **Good-quality lip balm with SPF.** Useful for the walk to the car or bus in the summer sun.
18. **A box of local treats.** Choose something individually wrapped so it can be shared, and think about common allergies and dietary needs. If in doubt, look for clear labelling.
19. **A gift card for a local café near the office.** It's still thoughtful, especially if it's the one everyone goes to.

## If you want it to feel personal

20. **Their initial on something useful**, such as a mug, a notebook or a tote bag. It's personal without being too personal.
21. **Something linked to a passion they've mentioned.** If they've mentioned their footy team, their dog or a hobby in a team meeting, a small related item shows you were listening.

## What to skip

- **Anything scented and strong**, like candles, diffusers and perfume. Shared offices and sensitive noses don't mix.
- **Joke gifts that could embarrass anyone.** What's funny between friends can land badly at work.
- **Alcohol**, unless you know your colleague drinks and your workplace is fine with it.
- **Clothing**, because sizing is a minefield.
- **Self-improvement hints** like diet books, alarm clocks, "World's Okayest Employee" mugs or anything that suggests they need fixing.

## Little things that make it nicer

- **Do a bit of quiet research.** Ask a close teammate, look at what's already on their desk, or listen in on morning chat.
- **Keep the receipt or ask for a gift receipt**, and pop it in the card if appropriate.
- **Wrap it**, even simply. Brown paper and string look lovely and cost little.
- **Write a short card.** "Merry Christmas — hope this makes the 3pm slump a bit nicer" makes even a simple gift feel considered.
- **Shop early.** In the last weeks before Christmas, deliveries slow down and popular items sell out.

The best Secret Santa gifts aren't the most expensive. They're the ones that quietly get used every day in January. Choose something useful and kind, and you're done.
