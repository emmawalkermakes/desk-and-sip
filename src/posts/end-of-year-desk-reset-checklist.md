There's a particular kind of dread in sitting down at your desk on the first working day of January to find a cold mug, a pile of unopened mail, 400 unread emails and a sticky note that just says "CALL HIM". Future you deserves better.

This checklist is meant for the last day or two before you finish up for the year, whether you're at home or in an office. Work through it top to bottom. Most of it takes a few minutes per item, and the whole lot fits into a slow afternoon.

## Part 1: Loose ends (do this first, while your brain still works)

- [ ] **Write a "where I'm up to" note.** One page or one document: projects in progress, what's next on each, and who's waiting on what. Save it somewhere you'll see it first thing in January.
- [ ] **Clear or delegate anything urgent.** If something genuinely can't wait three weeks, hand it to someone who'll be working, and tell them clearly.
- [ ] **Book in January catch-ups now.** Calendars fill fast once everyone's back.
- [ ] **Turn sticky notes into tasks.** Anything worth keeping goes into your task list or calendar. The paper notes go in the recycling.
- [ ] **Chase anything you're owed** (invoices, approvals or replies) so it doesn't sit untouched over the break.

## Part 2: Digital tidy-up

- [ ] **Set your out-of-office.** Include the dates you're away, the date you're back, and who to contact for anything urgent. Keep it short.
- [ ] **Update your calendar status** so people don't book meetings during your leave.
- [ ] **Triage your inbox.** You don't need inbox zero. Archive anything finished, and flag the handful of emails you'll need in January.
- [ ] **Clear your desktop and downloads folder.** File what you need, delete what you don't, and empty the bin.
- [ ] **Back up your work.** Check that your files are syncing to whatever backup or cloud storage your workplace uses. If you're self-employed, this is the moment to make a fresh backup of anything important.
- [ ] **Install pending updates** and restart your computer before you leave, not on your first morning back.
- [ ] **Unsubscribe from five newsletters** you never read. Future you will notice the difference.

## Part 3: Physical clean

Heat, humidity and three weeks of a closed room aren't kind to a neglected desk. A bit of cleaning now heads off smells, mould spots and ants.

- [ ] **Take home or empty every cup, bottle and container.** This is the most important item on the list.
- [ ] **Remove all food.** Yes, including the emergency muesli bar in the drawer. Ants find it over summer.
- [ ] **Turn your keyboard upside down** and gently tap out the crumbs. Follow your device maker's cleaning instructions for anything beyond a dry cloth.
- [ ] **Wipe down your desk, mouse, phone and headset.** These are the surfaces you touch most.
- [ ] **Clean your screen** with a soft, dry microfibre cloth, or whatever your screen's maker recommends.
- [ ] **Water your plants, or find them a holiday home.** A good soak before you go, out of direct sun, helps hardy plants get through a short break. Thirstier ones may be happier with a neighbour or a colleague who's working.
- [ ] **Deal with the paper pile.** Shred anything sensitive, file what you need and recycle the rest.

## Part 4: Power and safety

- [ ] **Switch off and unplug** monitors, lamps, desk fans, chargers and speakers at the wall. It saves standby power and is one less thing to worry about during summer storms.
- [ ] **Take your laptop home or lock it away**, according to your workplace's rules.
- [ ] **Close the blinds** if your desk gets afternoon sun. It keeps heat off screens and anything that might fade.
- [ ] **Check the window is shut and locked** if you work from home and you're going away.

## Part 5: Set up January you

This is the part that turns a clean desk into a good first day back.

- [ ] **Leave your "where I'm up to" note** on the keyboard or as your first calendar event.
- [ ] **Block out your first morning back** for catching up. No meetings, if you can manage it.
- [ ] **Restock the basics**: pens that work, a notebook with blank pages, tissues, lip balm and a charging cable that actually reaches.
- [ ] **Leave one nice thing out.** A clean mug, your favourite tea or a fresh notebook. It's small, but it helps.

## The 15-minute version

Only got a quarter of an hour before you're out the door? Do these five:

1. Out-of-office on.
2. Cups, bottles and food gone.
3. Quick "where I'm up to" note.
4. Everything switched off at the wall.
5. Plants watered.

## Print it, tick it, go

Copy this list into your notes app or print it out and keep it in your desk drawer. It works for any long break, not just Christmas. Then log off properly. The desk will still be there in January, and it'll be much nicer to come back to.
