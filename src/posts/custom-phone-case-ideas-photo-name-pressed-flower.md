You look at your phone more than almost anything else you own, so a case you actually like is a small daily pleasure. A custom case is also a popular gift because it's personal, useful and doesn't take up space.

This guide covers three of the most popular personalised styles (**photo**, **name** and **pressed flower**) with ideas, design tips and practical checks so the case fits, protects and still looks good after a summer in a beach bag.

## First: get the model exactly right

The most common custom-case mistake is the wrong model. Phone models in the same family often have slightly different sizes and camera layouts.

- **Check Settings.** On an iPhone, go to Settings > General > About. On Android, look under Settings > About phone.
- **Note the full name**, including "Plus", "Pro", "Max", "Ultra", "FE" or "Lite".
- **If it's a gift**, sneak a look at their settings, or borrow the phone "to check something".

## Choose the case type

| Case type | Protection | Look | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slim hard case | Light | Sleek | Good for printing; minimal drop protection |
| Flexible (TPU) case | Moderate | Slightly thicker | Grippy, absorbs small knocks; clear versions can yellow over time |
| Tough or dual-layer case | High | Bulkier | Best for drop-prone owners and tradies |
| Clear case with insert | Varies | Shows a printed or pressed design behind clear plastic | Easy to change the design |
| Wallet or folio case | Moderate to high | Book-style | Personalise the cover; good for cards |

Ask whether the case supports **wireless charging** and **MagSafe** or magnetic accessories if the owner uses them.

## Style 1: Photo cases

A photo case is the classic sentimental gift. Done well, it's lovely; done badly, it's a blurry rectangle.

### Photo ideas

- The dog or cat, ideally close-up and front-on.
- Kids or grandkids, especially candid shots.
- A favourite beach, view or holiday moment.
- A collage of four small photos from the year.
- A child's drawing, scanned in good light.

### Getting the photo right

- **Use the original** from the camera roll, not a screenshot or a photo saved from social media, which are often compressed.
- **Check resolution.** A phone case print is roughly 7 × 15 cm. Use the [photo size checker](/photo-size-checker/) to see if your photo is big enough.
- **Mind the camera cut-out.** The top corner of the case has a hole for the camera. Keep faces and important details away from that area.
- **Choose simple backgrounds.** Busy backgrounds compete with the subject on a small surface.
- **Bright, even light** prints better than dark or backlit shots.

## Style 2: Name and lettering cases

Name cases range from bold and graphic to tiny and minimal.

### Ideas

- **Small initials** near the bottom edge on a solid colour: understated and smart.
- **Full name in large type** running vertically: modern and bold.
- **Name over a pattern**: a stripe, check, terrazzo or botanical background with a name in a clear panel.
- **Handwriting**: a name or short note in someone's own handwriting, scanned. Lovely for a gift from a partner or child.
- **Name plus emoji or icon**: a wave, a paw, a coffee cup.

### Design tips

- **Contrast matters.** White text on a pale pattern disappears.
- **Avoid tiny script fonts.** They can blur when printed small.
- **Leave space** around the camera cut-out and the edges, which curve on many cases.
- **Spell-check**, then get someone else to.

## Style 3: Pressed-flower cases

Pressed-flower cases have a soft, botanical look. They come in two main types: **real pressed flowers** sealed in resin or under a clear back, and **printed** designs that recreate the pressed-flower look.

### Real pressed flowers

- **Pros:** genuinely one-of-a-kind; can include flowers from a wedding bouquet or garden.
- **Cons:** can be thicker; colours may fade over time with sun exposure; harder to repair if damaged.
- **Australian heat:** resin and some adhesives can soften or yellow if left in direct sun or a hot car. Keep the phone out of the dashboard sun.

### Printed pressed-flower designs

- **Pros:** slimmer, more consistent, often more durable; easy to add a name.
- **Cons:** not the real thing, if that matters to the recipient.

### Ideas

- Native wildflowers: wattle, boronia, everlasting daisies.
- Flowers from a wedding bouquet, pressed soon after the day.
- A small sprig of each family member's birth flower.
- A pressed-flower border with a name in the centre.

### Pressing your own flowers

If you want to press flowers yourself for a case insert:

1. Pick flowers that are naturally flat or small (pansies, violas, small daisies, ferns).
2. Pick on a dry day, after any dew has gone.
3. Press between sheets of baking paper inside a heavy book, with more books on top.
4. Leave for about two weeks, checking for mould in humid weather. Swap the paper if it gets damp.

## Care and longevity

- **Keep cases out of direct sun** for long periods; strong UV can fade prints and yellow clear plastics.
- **Clean gently** with a soft cloth and mild soap. Avoid harsh solvents, which can damage printed or coated finishes.
- **Remove sand** before putting the case back on after a beach day. Sand trapped between case and phone can scratch.
- **Check for wear** at the edges. If the case is cracking, replace it before it fails you on a drop.

## Gift notes

A custom case is personal, but it's also a bit of a gamble on taste. If you're unsure:

- Go for a **subtle design** (small initials, a soft pattern) over something bold.
- Include a note saying you're happy to swap it if they'd prefer a different design, if the seller allows.
- Or give a voucher with a mock-up so they can choose the final design.

Whichever style you pick, the best custom cases are the ones that make the owner smile when they pick up their phone, which, let's face it, is often.
