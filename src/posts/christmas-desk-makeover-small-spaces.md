If you work from a corner of the bedroom, a share-house desk or a hot-desk at the office, you probably don't have room for a full Christmas tree. But a desk is the perfect size for a small festive refresh, and personalised touches make it feel like *your* space rather than a generic display.

Here's how to plan a Christmas desk makeover that's festive, functional and doesn't eat into the space you need to actually work, with ideas suited to small spaces and a hot Australian December.

## Step 1: Clear the decks

Before adding anything, take everything off the desk. Wipe it down, untangle the cables, and only put back what you use daily. A festive desk looks best when it isn't already cluttered.

Quick wins:

- A **cable tray or clips** under the desk.
- A **small tray** for pens, keys and bits.
- A **desk mat** to define your work area.

## Step 2: Pick a small colour plan

Choosing two or three colours stops a small space looking busy. Some Aussie-summer-friendly palettes:

- **Coastal Christmas:** white, sand, seafoam and a touch of gold.
- **Native Christmas:** eucalyptus green, wattle yellow and warm red.
- **Classic but light:** red and green with plenty of white space.
- **Modern minimal:** black, white and one metallic.

Stick to your palette for everything you add, including personalised items.

## Step 3: Add a few personalised pieces

Personalised items add warmth without adding clutter, because each one has a job.

### A personalised desk mat

A felt or leather-look desk mat with your initials makes the whole desk look considered. Choose a neutral colour and add festive pieces on top.

### A name tumbler or mug

A tumbler with your name keeps iced drinks cold through a hot December afternoon. In a shared office, a name means it comes back to your desk. If you're more of a hot-coffee person, a personalised mug in your palette works the same way.

### A small photo block or frame

One good photo beats five small ones. An acrylic photo block of family, friends or a pet from this year's Christmas is a nice desk-sized keepsake. Check your photo is sharp enough with the [photo size checker](/photo-size-checker/).

### A personalised mini tree or ornament stand

A tabletop tree under 30 cm, with a few ornaments bearing names of the people you live or work with, is festive without taking over. Alternatively, a small stand or hook with a single personalised ornament.

### A name-labelled pen pot or organiser

Practical and personal. Choose one in your palette and fill it with a couple of red and green pens for the season.

### A custom phone stand

If your phone sits on the desk all day, a small stand with your initials keeps it upright for calls and off your workspace.

## Step 4: Add soft festive touches

These don't need to be personalised. They just add mood:

- **A small sprig of native greenery** in a bud vase: eucalyptus lasts well and smells lovely.
- **Battery-operated fairy lights** around a monitor stand or shelf. Choose warm white for a softer look.
- **A Christmas card display** using a string and pegs above the desk, which uses wall space instead of desk space.
- **A small bowl of baubles** in your palette.

### Renter-friendly options

- **Removable hooks and strips** for lights and card strings. Test on an inconspicuous spot first; some can lift paint.
- **Over-the-door hooks** for a small wreath.
- **Pegboard** leaning against the wall rather than screwed in.
- **Washi tape** for cards, which usually peels away cleanly.

## Step 5: Think about the heat

A December desk in Australia can get warm, especially near windows. A few things to watch:

- **Candles:** real candles soften in heat and are a fire risk near paper. Choose flameless LED candles.
- **Chocolate advent calendars:** keep them away from sunny windows or they'll melt.
- **Plants:** keep potted plants out of hot afternoon sun on a windowsill. Hardy indoor plants like ZZ plants and snake plants cope better with air-conditioned offices.
- **Tech:** keep laptops ventilated. Don't let decorations block vents or pile up around chargers.
- **Fairy lights:** use LED lights, which run cooler, and switch them off when you leave.

## Step 6: Keep it functional

Ask yourself: can I still work here? A festive desk should still have:

- A clear space for your keyboard, mouse and a notebook.
- Room for your drink that isn't next to the laptop.
- Easy reach to the things you use most.

If something gets in the way twice, move it.

## A sample small-desk setup

For a desk around 100 cm wide:

- **Left back corner:** mini tree (under 30 cm) with personalised ornaments.
- **Centre back:** monitor or laptop stand, with LED fairy lights draped behind.
- **Right back corner:** bud vase with eucalyptus, photo block in front.
- **Right front:** name tumbler on a coaster.
- **Front centre:** personalised desk mat with keyboard and notebook.
- **Wall above:** card string with pegs.

## Packing away

After the holidays, keep the personalised pieces that work all year (desk mat, tumbler, phone stand, pen pot) and pack away the seasonal ones. Store ornaments and lights in a cool, dry cupboard rather than a hot garage, where heat can damage plastics and adhesives.

## The takeaway

A small festive desk is about restraint: a clear base, a tight colour plan, a few personalised pieces that you'll use, and some soft seasonal touches. It's a nice way to make the last weeks of the year at your desk feel a bit more like Christmas.
