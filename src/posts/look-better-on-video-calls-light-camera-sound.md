End-of-year video calls pile up fast: reviews, planning meetings, client catch-ups, the virtual team Christmas drinks. If you've ever caught sight of your own tile and thought "is that really what I look like?", the good news is that most of the problem is set-up, not you.

You don't need a ring light or a new webcam to look and sound noticeably better. Here are the fixes in order of impact.

## 1. Fix the light first

Lighting makes more difference than any camera upgrade.

**Face the light.** The best free light source is a window *in front* of you, behind your screen. Daylight falling on your face looks natural and flattering.

**Don't sit with a window behind you.** Your camera will expose for the bright window and turn you into a dark silhouette. If you can't move your desk, close the blind behind you or turn your chair.

**Avoid harsh overhead light alone.** A single ceiling light directly above you casts shadows under your eyes. Add a lamp in front of you, slightly to one side, to fill those in.

**Soften it.** A lamp pointed at a white wall in front of you gives a softer light than one pointed straight at your face. Even a sheet of white paper or a light-coloured shirt on your lap bounces a little light upward.

**Watch for summer glare.** In Queensland the afternoon sun can swing across a room quickly. If your face goes from nicely lit to blown-out halfway through a meeting, a sheer curtain or a partially closed blind will diffuse it.

## 2. Raise the camera to eye level

Most laptop cameras sit low, so the other person looks up your nose and at the ceiling behind you.

Put the laptop on a stand, a sturdy box or a stack of books, so the camera is roughly level with your eyes. If you use a separate keyboard and mouse, this is easy. If you don't, raise it for calls and lower it again afterwards.

This is also better for your neck, which you'll appreciate by Friday.

## 3. Frame yourself properly

- **Head and shoulders in shot,** with a little space above your head. Not just a forehead, not your whole torso.
- **Sit an arm's length or so from the camera.** Too close distorts your face; too far makes you look small and far away.
- **Look at the camera when you're speaking,** at least sometimes. It's the closest thing to eye contact on a call. Moving the window with the speaker's video close to the camera helps.

## 4. Tidy what's behind you

Look at what your camera actually sees, not what you see from the chair.

- **Clear the obvious clutter** in frame: washing, dishes, piles of paper.
- **A plain wall, a bookshelf or a plant** all make calm backgrounds.
- **Mind what's on the screen behind you** if there's a TV or second monitor in shot.
- **Background blur** is built into most video apps and is a perfectly good option for a busy room. It can blur hands and hair edges, though, so a tidy real background still looks better.

## 5. Sound matters more than picture

People will forgive a slightly grainy picture. They won't forgive echoing, crackly or muffled sound.

- **Use a headset or earphones with a microphone.** Almost anything with a mic near your mouth beats a built-in laptop microphone, and wearing earphones stops your speakers echoing back into the call.
- **Soften the room.** Hard, empty rooms echo. Rugs, curtains, bookshelves and soft furnishings reduce it. If you're in a bare room, even a throw over a chair nearby helps.
- **Mute when you're not talking,** especially in larger meetings. Learn the mute keyboard shortcut in your app.
- **Turn off notifications** on your computer so chimes don't come through.
- **Watch the fan.** A desk fan or air-conditioner blowing near the mic can make a rushing sound. Point it away from you during calls, or turn it down.

## 6. Steady the connection

A frozen picture or robotic voice is usually a network problem.

- **Use a cable if you can.** An Ethernet cable from your router to your computer is usually more stable than Wi-Fi.
- **Sit closer to the router** if you're on Wi-Fi, or with fewer walls in between.
- **Pause big downloads** and cloud backups during important calls.
- **Turn your video off** if the connection is struggling. Clear sound is more important than a picture.
- **Close tabs and apps** you don't need. Video calls use a lot of your computer's resources.

## 7. Do a two-minute test before important calls

Most video apps have a preview or test function. Before a call that matters:

- [ ] Check the light on your face (no silhouette, no harsh shadows)
- [ ] Camera at eye level, head and shoulders in frame
- [ ] Background tidy, or blur on
- [ ] Headset connected and selected as the microphone
- [ ] Notifications off
- [ ] Water nearby (in a cup with a lid, well away from the laptop)

## A note on those end-of-year online parties

Virtual team gatherings are more relaxed, but the basics still help: good light, a decent mic, and a check of what's behind you. If you're in a room with family around over the holidays, a quick "I'm on a call" signal on the door can save a surprise cameo.

None of these fixes is about looking perfect. They're about making it easy for the people on the other end to see and hear you, so the conversation can get on with being useful.
